`createJsonlTransport` is now the one owner of its child process and reports how it ended as a structured, replayable fact instead of an error string. It records three facts, each once: how the child ended, whether and when a stop was requested, and how the channel ended. From their order it derives the cause (`requested`, `unexpected` or `spawn-failed`) and reports the end exactly once, after the last stdout message was delivered:

- `ended: Promise<TransportEnd>` fulfils exactly once and never rejects. It carries the cause, the process exit (`exited` with separate `code` and `signal`, `spawn-failed`, or `unobserved`), the drain outcome (`complete`; `timed-out`, with `stdoutComplete` telling a held stderr from a truncated stdout; `failed`; or `abandoned` when the exit was not observed and no drain window opened; each with the number of dropped characters), the first communication failure (`stdin-failed`, `stdout-failed`, `stdout-ended` or the new `listener-failed`), and evidence about the child's process group (`tree`: `not-owned`, `quiescent` or `unproven` with a reason), probed after every end of an owned group, requested or not.
- `exitObserved: Promise<ObservedProcessExit>` fulfils only when an exit or a failed spawn was really observed, also when that happens after an end reported the exit as `unobserved`.
- `status` (`writable`, `ending`, `ended`) is synchronous. Writing is blocked from the stop intent, the observed exit or the first channel failure on; `send()` then throws at once. A stdin write error (`EPIPE`), a stdout error or, under `listenerErrors: 'fail'`, a throwing message listener fails the channel and stops the child.
- After the exit, stdout and stderr are drained for at most `drainTimeoutMs` (default 5000). A pipe held open by a descendant ends the drain as `timed-out` and is released; the observed exit is reported separately. A child that never started reports a `complete` drain on every runtime.
- `onStdoutEnd` is required: `terminal` waits up to the drain window for the exit once stdout ended, then fails the channel (`stdout-ended`) and stops the child; `ignore` keeps supervising until the child exits or is stopped. `finalLine: 'parse'` parses an unterminated final line at the genuine stdout end.
- New options: `killGraceMs` (default 5000), `exitObservationMs` (default 2000), `terminateTree` (default `true`), `envMode` (`merge` or `replace`), `stderr` (`log` or `suppress`), `listenerErrors` (`isolate` or `fail`), `maxLineLength` and an injectable `signalPort`. The three timings and `drainTimeoutMs` form the exported `ProcessStopTiming`, which the process lifecycle and `StdioServerTransport` accept as well.
- `mayHaveLeftProcesses(end)` tells whether an end may have left a process of the child running: an unobserved exit, a pipe held past the drain deadline, or a process group not proven quiescent.
- Runtime limits: under a Bun host a write to a stdin whose reader closed reports success and emits no error, so `stdin-failed` is only raised on Node; the channel then ends with the child's exit, its stdout end or `close()`. The Windows tree stop (`taskkill /T /F`, one forceful stage, bounded by the exit observation and the caller's deadline) has test cases that run only on a Windows host, and no Windows CI job runs them yet, so that path is unverified.

`createProcessLifecycle` uses `onStdoutEnd: 'ignore'`, and `StdioServerTransport` uses `onStdoutEnd: 'terminal'`. `StdioServerTransport.isReady()` is `false` once its session stops accepting writes, and it no longer replies to a handshake that arrives after its child exited. `createJsonRpcClient` takes the new `JsonlChannel` (the message channel `IJsonlTransport` extends) and drops a server-request response that can no longer be written instead of raising it from its dispatch.

**Breaking:**

- `IJsonlTransport.close()` is asynchronous and bounded. It no longer clears the listeners and kills the child at once: it records the stop intent, sends `SIGTERM` while the exit is unobserved, escalates to `SIGKILL` after `killGraceMs` on POSIX (Windows terminates forcefully in one stage), waits at most `exitObservationMs`, and returns `ended`. A child whose exit is not observed in time ends as `unobserved`, never as an invented exit. `close({ deadline })` shortens every phase to the caller's deadline, including the quiescence proof of an end that is already decided. Messages still in the pipe are delivered until the end report.
- `createJsonlTransport` spawns its child as the leader of an isolated POSIX process group by default (`terminateTree: true`), and a stop signals the whole group while the child's exit is unobserved. Group members that outlive the child's observed exit are not signalled again, because a group ID is reserved only while the group has a member and a probe cannot prove that the group it finds is still the owner's; the end report shows them as `tree: unproven`. Processes that leave the group (`setsid`, daemonising tools, or a nested owner that uses this default for its own child) are neither stopped nor counted. The child runs in a session of its own: signals aimed at the host's terminal job (Ctrl-C, `SIGQUIT`, a terminal hangup) no longer reach it, it has no controlling terminal, and a host that dies without stopping it leaves it running, so such a child must exit by itself when its stdin closes. Pass `terminateTree: false` to keep the previous direct-child ownership. This applies to `createProcessLifecycle` and `StdioServerTransport` children as well.
- `createJsonlTransport` requires `onStdoutEnd`; `IJsonlTransport` gains `ended`, `exitObserved` and `status`; `send()` throws once writing is blocked instead of writing to a closed pipe.
- `createJsonRpcClient` takes a `JsonlChannel` instead of an `IJsonlTransport`. Every `IJsonlTransport` is one.
