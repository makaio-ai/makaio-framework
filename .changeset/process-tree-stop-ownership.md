`@makaio/subprocess` gains one helper for stopping the processes a child starts, so owners no longer keep their own copies of group signalling and quiescence checks. `spawnOptionsForOwnership('direct-child' | 'process-group')` returns the spawn options for an ownership; `process-group` isolates the child in a POSIX process group of its own (`detached: true`). `signalOwnedProcesses` signals the child or its whole group and returns a checked result: `delivered`, `absent` (`ESRCH`) or `denied` (`EPERM`, inconclusive on its own), and throws any other error. It refuses PIDs that would address the host's own group or every process. `proveProcessGroupQuiescence` proves within a deadline that no live member is left in a group, counting unreaped zombies as gone, and otherwise reports why the proof failed (`poll-timeout`, `ps-unavailable`, `signal-error`); an `AbortSignal` ends it early. `terminateWindowsProcessTree` ends a Windows process tree forcefully in one stage with `taskkill /PID <pid> /T /F`, checks its exit code and rejects with a `WindowsTreeTerminationError` that tells a timed-out run from a failed one. The POSIX signal primitive is an injectable `ProcessSignalPort`; `taskkill` does not use it.

Process-group evidence covers the group only: a descendant that leaves it (`setsid`, `setpgid`, a daemonising tool, or a nested owner that isolates its own child in a group of its own) is neither signalled with the group nor counted by the proof.

The workspace setup command now runs on this helper. Its result types are unchanged, and it still rejects Windows hosts because the quiescence proof is POSIX-only. Its stop is now bounded in every phase: a failed `SIGTERM` no longer skips the `SIGKILL`, and a command whose exit is not observed within 2 s of its `SIGKILL` (for example because its signals were denied) settles as `stop-failed` with the group evidence gathered then, instead of waiting for an exit that may never come. The native probe driver's `runCommand` settles every end the same way: it clears its timers, releases the pipes and unreferences a child whose exit was not observed, and its post-kill settle time starts once the tree termination (a bounded `taskkill`, a quiescence proof) has finished instead of running beside it.

Side effects of `process-group` ownership on POSIX: the child runs in a new session, leaves the host's foreground process group and has no controlling terminal. Signals aimed at the host's terminal job (Ctrl-C, `SIGQUIT`, a terminal hangup) no longer reach it, so a host that dies without stopping the group leaves the child running until it exits by itself.
