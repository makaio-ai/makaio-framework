After an owner has observed the exit of the leader of a POSIX process group, the group gets no further signal. The rule is the same for the JSONL transport and process lifecycle and the native probe driver, and it is stated once in `@makaio/subprocess` (`process-tree.ts`): a group ID is reserved only while the group has a member, and a probe (`kill(-pid, 0)`, the process table) cannot prove that the group it found is still the one the owner spawned when the next signal resolves the number again. A member that outlives the leader is reported, not stopped. The rule concerns POSIX group numbers only; the Windows tree termination (`taskkill /T`, addressed by PID) keeps its own contract. An exit the owner has not observed yet does not strictly mean the child is unreaped, because the runtime reaps a child before it dispatches the exit callback; the rule is the best evidence an owner can have, not a kernel guarantee.

**Behaviour change (native probe driver):** `runCommand` no longer kills the process group of a provider whose exit was observed before its deadline. It keeps the bounded settling of the pipes and proves the group quiescent instead. The result carries a new `cleanup` field, `{ kind: 'complete' }` or `{ kind: 'incomplete', reason }` (`pipes-held`, a quiescence gap such as `poll-timeout`, or `tree-not-addressable` on Windows, where the tree is likewise not terminated after the exit was observed, as before), and `test:agent-clients` reports an incomplete cleanup as a failure of the scenario. A custom `terminateTree` option must now return a `RunCleanup`.
